## Answer

* **Nationally, roughly half of the Q3 2026 Ram 1500 jump was non-retail.** Retail +42% against total +73% means retail supplied 40-52% of the added units for any plausible base mix. In Q2 2026 it was about a quarter. Q4 2025, the Hemi launch quarter, was retail-led.
* **The Hemi is a retail engine; the fleet volume is six-cylinder.** In NY, 91% of MY2026+ Hemi registrations carry retail plates vs 82% for the six-cylinder. Rental registrations are 8.2% of six-cylinder trucks but 0.5% of Hemis (19 of 5,236). Fleet growth therefore does not undercut the Hemi pricing-power argument, but it does mean headline Ram 1500 growth overstates individual-buyer demand for the truck overall.
* **In NY, Ram's retail registrations grew about as fast as its total** (+103% to +128% vs +112% to +133%). That is well ahead of F-150, Silverado, Sierra and Tundra, which face the same snapshot bias. Ram's retail share slipped from about 89% (2025) to 85-88% (2026) while rivals were flat at about 79-81%. NY is one state, and national fleet buyers (rental, utilities) often register out of state, so NY understates fleet.
* **Not available:** S&P Global Mobility / Experian national registrations by model and channel are licensed. Stellantis discloses fleet only as a share of total sales in some quarters (none for the Ram 1500 alone).
